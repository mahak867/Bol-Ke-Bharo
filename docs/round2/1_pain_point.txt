Millions of elderly people, widows and people with disabilities in India are entitled to a pension but cannot apply on their own. The portals are in English or formal Hindi, need typing, and reject any application where documents don't match exactly. So they go to a Common Service Centre operator, bank mitra or ASHA worker, who fills the form for them.

That is where it breaks. A 68-year-old woman explains her details in Awadhi. The operator translates in their head and types into an English portal. Nobody reads the form back to her, because she cannot read it. If her Aadhaar says "Ramvati" and her passbook says "Ram Wati", nobody notices until the application is rejected weeks later. She comes back again and again, losing a day's wages and bus fare each time, and the operator, who is paid per form, loses income too.

The pain is not filling in a form. It is that the conversation that holds all the information is spoken, the form is typed, and the errors in between are discovered far too late.
